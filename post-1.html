Ever tried to upload a photo and seen "file too large"? A free image compressor solves that, but most online tools make you guess. You drag a quality slider, download, check the size, and repeat until it fits.

The problem with most image compressors

Most tools only lower the JPEG quality. That works until you push it too far, and then text turns to mush and you get blocky artifacts. That's a real problem for screenshots, receipts, IDs, and scanned documents.

A better way to compress an image

photoscale is a free online image compressor that lets you pick the size you need instead of guessing. It runs a two-part search: first it finds the best quality at your image's original resolution, and if that can't hit the target, it scales the dimensions proportionally. The result is a file that lands under your limit while keeping edges and small text sharp.

How to compress an image to an exact size

  1. Open photoscale in your browser.
  2. Drag in your image, browse for it, or paste it with Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on Mac).
  3. Choose a preset (50 KB, 100 KB, 8 MB) or type a custom limit in KB or MB.
  4. Compare before and after, then click Save.

Common size limits and where they show up

  • 50 KB — visa, passport, and government ID photo portals
  • 100 KB — job application systems
  • 8 MB — Discord's free upload limit

Why "online" doesn't have to mean "uploaded"

Most online image compressors send your file to a server. photoscale processes everything in your browser using HTML5 Canvas. Your passport scan never leaves your device, which is why it's a safer choice for sensitive files.
